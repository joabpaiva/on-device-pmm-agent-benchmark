BATTLECARD: Aurora 14 vs Vantage Pro 16

POSITIONING
Aurora 14 is the lighter, more expandable option. It suits buyers who want on-device AI, room to grow and strong service coverage. Vantage Pro 16 costs less and has a larger, faster screen and more GPU memory, which suits visualization-heavy work.

AURORA 14 STRENGTHS
1. Memory: Up to 128 GB, user-upgradeable. Buyers can start at 32 GB and grow. Vantage maxes out at 64 GB, soldered.
2. On-device AI: 60 TOPS NPU vs 40 TOPS. It has enough memory headroom to run ~30B-parameter open models locally.
3. Portability and service: 1.65 kg vs 2.30 kg. It has two Thunderbolt 5 ports (Vantage has one Thunderbolt 4) and a 3-year on-site warranty (Vantage: 1-year depot).

VANTAGE PRO 16 STRENGTHS (be upfront about these)
1. Display: 16-inch, 165 Hz. Reviewers consistently praise it for visualization work.
2. GPU memory: 24 GB vs Aurora's 16 GB. This helps with large 3D scenes and bigger GPU-bound models.

LIKELY OBJECTIONS

1. "Vantage is $300 cheaper."
Response: That's correct ($2,899 vs $2,599). The Aurora price includes a 3-year on-site warranty instead of 1-year mail-in. It also includes memory you can upgrade to 128 GB and storage up to 4 TB (Vantage: 2 TB).

2. "Vantage has more GPU memory."
Response: It does, and that's an advantage for large 3D scenes. If the work is local AI models, Aurora's memory headroom and 60 TOPS NPU matter more. Two independent reviews also noted audible fan noise on Vantage under sustained GPU load.

3. "Vantage's battery lasts longer."
Response: Its rating is higher (13 h vs 11 h). Both figures are "up to" and measured on video playback. Aurora is 0.65 kg lighter, which helps buyers who carry it daily.

DISCOVERY QUESTION
"Over the next two years, will this machine's heaviest workload be large 3D visualization or running AI models locally?"
