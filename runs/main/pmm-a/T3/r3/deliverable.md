The Aurora 14 AI Workstation's performance depends on memory capacity and use case. The 8B model ran at comparable speeds against a competitor at 41 tokens/second. The 30B model required 128 GB memory for meaningful performance at 17 tokens/second—the 32 GB base configuration dropped to 6 tokens/second. Image generation lagged behind competitors at 5.3 seconds per image versus 4.1 seconds. Sustained 40-minute runs per formed ~10-12% slower than peaks as thermal limits approached. The AI-assisted design session stayed stable with 128 GB memory but added 8-second reload delays on the 32 GB base when the CAD assembly grew large. Battery life under AI inference was 2 hours 40 minutes, significantly below the 11-hour video playback rating measured at 150 nits brightness with Wi-Fi off. All Aurora 14 test units were pre-production before final firmware release, and the test sample size was three units per configuration.

- Memory capacity exceeding AI model requirements dictates performance rather than raw processor speed.
- GPU capability drives image generation speed, where Aurora 14 at 5.3s/img trails the competitor at 4.1s/img.
- Sustained performance degrades 9-12% over 40-minute runs as thermal limits are reached and battery life under AI workloads is 2h40m.
