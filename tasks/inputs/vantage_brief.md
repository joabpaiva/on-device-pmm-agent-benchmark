# Competitive Brief: Aurora 14 vs Vantage Pro 16

*Fictional products created for benchmark testing. Internal sales enablement material.*

## At a glance

| Attribute | Aurora 14 | Vantage Pro 16 |
|---|---|---|
| Starting price | $2,899 | $2,599 |
| Processor | Helix H9 AI, 16 cores | Corvex X12, 14 cores |
| NPU | 60 TOPS | 40 TOPS |
| GPU | Vireo V8000 Pro, 16 GB | Vireo V9000, 24 GB |
| Max memory | 128 GB, user-upgradeable (2 slots) | 64 GB, soldered (not upgradeable) |
| Max storage | 4 TB (2 × M.2) | 2 TB (1 × M.2) |
| Display | 14" OLED 2880 × 1800, 120 Hz | 16" IPS 2560 × 1600, 165 Hz |
| Weight | 1.65 kg | 2.30 kg |
| Battery | 86 Wh, up to 11 h video playback | 99 Wh, up to 13 h video playback |
| Thunderbolt | 2 × Thunderbolt 5 | 1 × Thunderbolt 4 |
| Standard warranty | 3-year on-site | 1-year depot (mail-in) |

## Where Vantage Pro 16 is genuinely strong
- Larger 16-inch screen with a 165 Hz refresh rate; reviewers consistently praise it for visualization work.
- More GPU memory (24 GB vs 16 GB), an advantage for large 3D scenes and bigger GPU-bound models.
- Longer rated battery life (13 h vs 11 h, both measured on video playback).
- Lower starting price ($300 less).

## Where Aurora 14 leads
- Memory: up to 128 GB and user-upgradeable, versus 64 GB soldered. Buyers can start at 32 GB and grow.
- AI on the device: 60 TOPS NPU versus 40 TOPS, and enough memory headroom to run ~30B-parameter open models locally.
- Portability: 0.65 kg lighter.
- Connectivity: two Thunderbolt 5 ports versus one Thunderbolt 4.
- Service: 3-year on-site warranty standard versus 1-year depot.

## Analyst and reviewer notes
- Two independent reviews noted audible fan noise on the Vantage Pro 16 under sustained GPU load.
- Analysts expect memory capacity to matter more as teams run larger AI models on the device.
